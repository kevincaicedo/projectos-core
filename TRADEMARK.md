# ProjectOS trademark policy

The code in `projectos-core` is licensed under Apache-2.0. The **name** is not.
Apache-2.0 §6 grants no trademark rights on purpose, and this document says
exactly what we do and do not permit, so that nobody has to guess.

Holder: **Private AI Inc.**

## The marks

- **ProjectOS** — the word mark, alone or in combination.
- **ProjectOS Cloud** — the hosted service.
- The ProjectOS logo and wordmark artwork in `assets/`.

## What you may do without asking

- Say truthfully that your work uses, builds on, integrates with, or is
  compatible with ProjectOS. "Built with ProjectOS", "works with ProjectOS",
  "a ProjectOS plugin" are all fine.
- Redistribute **unmodified** official builds under the ProjectOS name.
- Use the name in academic papers, talks, reviews, comparisons, tutorials, and
  news — including critical ones. Nominative and descriptive use is not
  something we get to license.
- Use the logo to link to the project, at its published proportions and
  colours, without implying that we endorse you.

## What needs a different name

- **Modified builds.** If you change the source and distribute the result, do
  not call the result ProjectOS. Call it something else and describe it as
  "based on ProjectOS" or "a fork of ProjectOS". This is the whole point of the
  policy: a user who downloads something called ProjectOS should be able to
  trust that it is the code we published.
- **Hosted services.** Do not offer a hosted service under a name containing
  "ProjectOS" or a confusingly similar variation. "ProjectOS Cloud" is reserved
  for the official service operated by Private AI Inc.
- **Products, companies, domains, and app-store listings** whose name contains
  ProjectOS or a near variation.
- **Merchandise** bearing the marks.

## What is never permitted

- Implying sponsorship, endorsement, affiliation, or certification by
  Private AI Inc. when none exists.
- Altering the logo's colours, proportions, or elements, or combining it into
  another mark.
- Using the marks in a way that suggests our code is responsible for behaviour
  it does not have — including security claims about a modified build.

## Asking

Requests, including for uses this document does not cover, go to
**ing.sys.kevincaicedo@gmail.com**. We would rather grant a clear permission
than have a good project quietly rename itself.

## Why this exists

L4 and L9 promise that a user can inspect, run, self-host, extend, and export
the complete local-first product without an account. That promise depends on
the name meaning one specific set of bytes. A trademark policy is how an
open-source project keeps its name honest without making its code less free;
it restricts naming, never use, modification, or redistribution of the code
itself. The reasoning, and the licence alternatives we rejected, are recorded in
ADR-0003 (core licence and contribution model).
